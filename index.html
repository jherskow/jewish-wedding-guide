Guide to the Perplexed

A first-timer's manual to a Jewish wedding

07.02.5787 / 18.10.2026

Parts

Please no phones during the ceremony.

KABALAT PANIM 3:00

Hebrew, lit. receiving faces

TISCH 3:30

Yiddish, lit. table

Traditionally the groom's table. There's some singing, sometimes toasts.

Traditionally where the ketubah (the marriage contract) is reviewed carefully by the couple and the witnesses, then signed by two witnesses.

BEDEKEN 4:15

Yiddish, lit. covering

The groom takes a look under the veil to make sure he's marrying the correct person, in memory of a famous biblical mix-up, then lowers the veil over the bride's face.

Both the ketubah, the traditional Jewish marriage contract, and the New York marriage license are signed by the couple.

CHUPPAH 4:30

Hebrew, lit. canopy

The couple are brought to the chuppah by their parents. The bride and groom circle each other, symbolizing building layers of protection around their new life together, and the ceremony begins.

The rings are placed on the index finger, not the ring finger, during the ceremony: all witnesses must plainly see it go on.

The ketubah is read aloud to all and handed over.

Seven traditional marriage blessings are said over a cup of wine. Different guests are traditionally honored with the different blessings.

A glass is broken. Everyone shouts 'mazel tov'. After a round of well-wishing, the crowd dances the couple over to the yichud room.

YICHUD

Hebrew, lit. seclusion

Josh and Alex spend their first few minutes alone together as a married couple. They get a moment to relax and take a breath. In ancient times, a little more went on in there.

SEUDAT SIMCHA

Hebrew, lit. a joyful meal

The reception begins with a first course. Courses will alternate with dancing.

HORA

Hebrew, a circle dance

Ten minutes of traditional gender-segregated dancing. Dance in circles, with the bride or groom respectively.

After ten minutes the barriers are removed. The couple are lifted for the traditional chair dance.

The couple are then sat next to each other and guests entertain them, to fulfill the religious obligation to bring joy and dance in front of the newlywed couple.

The final ten minutes of the set are free-form dancing with no divider, still to a mix of traditional and modern Jewish music.

continued

Main course.

RIKUDIM

Hebrew, lit. dancing

The band plays a traditional wedding set. If you keep dancing, they'll keep playing. The second set is the classics.

KINUACH

Hebrew, lit. dessert

Dessert is out. The couple cut the cake, with a final toast.

BIRKAT HAMAZON

Hebrew, lit. grace after meals

The traditional Jewish grace after meals is said together, after which the seven blessings are recited again. There are booklets on the tables, which you are free to take home.

Keep this manual for future reference. Thank you for joining us on our day of joy.
